Which question is a sliding-window problem?

"Run of consecutive" is the signal: the answer is a contiguous range. The other questions ignore positions (frequency counter) or search sorted data (binary search).

 How many different 7-day periods does a list of 30 days contain?

A period can start at day 1 up to day 24 (days 24 to 30 is the last one): n − k + 1 = 30 − 7 + 1 = 24.

 What does recomputing the sum of every window of size k cost on n values?

There are n − k + 1 windows and each costs k additions. With k = 720 hours over 43,800 hours that is about 31 million additions.

 The window over days 10 to 16 totals T. What is the total for days 11 to 17?

The two windows share days 11 to 16. Day 10 leaves, day 17 enters: one subtraction and one addition, whatever the window size.

 How many operations does the fixed-size sliding window make on 43,800 hourly values with a 720-hour window?

The first window costs k, each of the remaining n − k slides costs one update: n in total, independent of the window size.

 A fixed window of size k ends at position right (inclusive). Where does it start?

Check a tiny case: k = 3, right = 3 covers positions 1, 2, 3, so it starts at 1 = 3 − 3 + 1. Position right − k is the one that just left.

 There are only 5 days of data and the owner asks for the best 7 days. What should bestPeriod return?

Returning the 5-day total silently answers a different question. Make the missing answer visible.

 bestPeriod updates the best with if (total > best.total). Two periods tie for the highest total. Which one is reported?

Strict > keeps the first best; >= would keep the last. Choose on purpose and document it.

 The revenue table has no rows for days the shop was closed. What goes wrong with a 7-row window?

Fill closed days with a 0 revenue row so that positions line up with days, or slide over dates (SQL RANGE with an interval) instead of rows.

 In the "at most one late delivery" streak, the window just became invalid (two lates). Why can left move forward for good?

It is the same argument as in two pointers: each move discards a candidate that cannot win. That is why the window never needs to move backwards.

 The variable window has a while loop inside a for loop. Why is it still O(n)?

Count total pointer moves, not loop nesting. This "amortised" argument is the key to every variable-window solution.

 A developer shrinks the streak window with if (late > maxLate) instead of while. What can go wrong?

The late delivery that must leave may be several positions in. Keep shrinking until the rule holds again.

 In the longest-block-without-repeat solution, why check previous >= left before jumping left?

With "abba", the last a's earlier position is 0 while the window starts at 2. Jumping to 1 would put both bs in the window.

 prefix[i] is the total of the first i values, with prefix[0] = 0. What is the total of positions from to to, inclusive?

Everything up to and including to is prefix[to + 1]; subtract everything before from, which is prefix[from]. The leading 0 makes ranges starting at 0 work with no special case.

 When are prefix sums a better fit than a sliding window?

Build once in O(n), then answer each range in O(1). A window only helps when consecutive ranges overlap as it slides.

 A past day's revenue is corrected after the prefix array was built. What does the update cost?

Prefix sums suit static data. For frequent updates and queries, Fenwick trees or segment trees do both in O(log n).

 Why does "shrink while the total is too big" fail on net cash flows that can be negative?

Use prefix sums with a Map of earlier prefix sums: a range from i to j has the target total exactly when prefix[i] = prefix[j + 1] − target.

 Why does countPeriods start its Map with 0 → 1?

A period from day 0 to day j has total prefix[j + 1] − 0. Without the initial 0 entry, those periods would be missed.

 Why can't a running maximum be updated like a running total when a day leaves the window?

Sums are invertible (subtract what leaves); maximums are not. The decreasing deque keeps exactly the candidates that could become the maximum later.

 In the window-maximum deque, a new day with revenue 90 arrives and the back of the deque holds a day with 75. Why is the 75 day removed for good?

Each position is pushed once and popped at most once, which makes the whole algorithm O(n).

 Which SQL computes a 7-row rolling revenue total inside the database?

Window functions are the database's sliding window. ROWS counts rows; use a date RANGE if days can be missing.

 For "the longest valid window" you shrink while the window is invalid. For "the shortest window whose total reaches ₦1,000,000" (non-negative revenues), when do you shrink?

For "shortest", every valid window is a candidate and the goal is to make it smaller, so you shrink while the rule still holds. For "longest", you shrink only to repair a broken rule.

 A rate limiter allows 100 requests per user in any 60-second period. Which idea is it built on?

Old requests leave the window as time passes, just as old days leave a 7-day window. Limiters keep either the timestamps in the window (exact) or counts per time bucket (approximate, less memory).
