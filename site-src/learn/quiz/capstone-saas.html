Why does the capstone build one vertical slice (a parent pays a term fee) end to end before anything else?

A vertical slice runs through HTTP, identity, tenancy, permissions, the provider, the database, events, jobs and observability at once. If those pieces fit together for the riskiest flow, the rest is incremental work with known acceptance criteria.

 Ada's phone sends the same payment request twice because of a weak network. What makes her be charged once?

Idempotency is a protocol between client and server (the key) and again between server and provider (the reference). The unique constraint makes it hold even when two copies race.

 KoboPay does not answer within the timeout on any of the three attempts. What does FeesDesk answer, and why?

A timeout means "no answer", not "no". Reporting failure could make the parent pay again and be charged twice; reporting success could credit money that never arrived. Pending is the only honest state.

 Why is the card charged between two transactions instead of inside one?

Rolling back a database transaction does not un-charge a card. So FeesDesk records "pending" first, charges, then settles in a second short transaction. A crash in between leaves a pending payment with a reference that reconciliation can finish.

 Two phones pay the full ₦450,000 for the same bill at the same moment. What stops the school receiving ₦900,000?

Subtracting pending payments is what closes the race: the first payment is not yet paid when the second request checks. The CHECK (paid_kobo <= amount_kobo) constraint is the last line of defence if the code is ever wrong.

 Tunde, from another school, tries to pay Ada's bill by changing the id in the URL. Why does he get 404 rather than 403?

requireAccess first asks whether the caller may read the bill. If not, it is "not found", exactly like a missing one. Only someone who can see the bill gets the more precise 403 for an action they may not take.

 Which facts does FeesDesk take from the payment request itself?

Validation proves a value has the right shape, not that the caller is entitled to it. Identity and tenant come from the server's own session record; money owed comes from the database.

 Which KoboPay outcomes does chargeWithRetry retry?

Retrying a permanent failure wastes time and can hide a configuration bug; retrying a decline is rude to the parent's bank. The retryIf predicate encodes which failures are transient.

 Why must all three attempts of one charge send the same reference?

In the provider example, the slow charge timed out three times and KoboPay still made exactly one charge. Idempotent references are what make retries safe for money.

 Why is fee.paid published after the settle transaction commits, not inside it?

Side effects follow facts. The remaining risk, a crash between commit and publish, is what an outbox table fixes (Milestone 3).

 With a plain tx.afterCommit(…), the receipt job kept failing with "Transaction is closed". What was happening?

AsyncLocalStorage context flows into everything started from a callback, including timers and queued jobs. Running the callback through context.exit starts that work with no transaction in scope. It is reported as a defect of the published package.

 Why does a receipt job carry only { schoolId, paymentId }, and call identity.runAs(schoolId, …)?

Every piece of background work needs its tenant restored the way a request gets it from the session. Carrying ids instead of copies also means a retried job sees the latest state.

 The queue may run the receipt job twice (a retry after a crash). Why is that harmless?

At-least-once delivery is the norm; design handlers so a repeat changes nothing important. Deterministic keys are the cheapest way.

 What is the difference between /health and /ready in FeesDesk?

A load balancer routes by readiness; an orchestrator restarts by liveness. Mixing them makes a slow dependency restart healthy processes.

 The lifecycle starts database, then receipts and scheduler, then http. In which order does it stop, and why?

The acceptance suite proves it: a payment in progress during shutdown still answers 201.

 Why does app.ts pass { scope: ContainerScope.SINGLETON } to registerFactory?

Without the scope, billing would get a second identity service with its own tenant manager. Know the default of every registration you write.

 Reminders should go out at 08:00 in Lagos. Why is the cron expression "0 7 * * 1-5" with timezone: "UTC"?

This conversion is only safe because Lagos has no daylight saving. For a zone that changes its offset, you would need a scheduler with real time-zone support or a job that checks the local time itself.

 The login limit test passed, yet the lesson calls the design bad. Why?

A test can pass and still describe the wrong behaviour. Count failures per account (school and e-mail), clear them on success, as in exercise 1.

 What does the cache key bills.greenfield.u-ada with the tag bills.greenfield achieve?

Tenant ids belong in cache keys for the same reason they belong in queries. (The first version used colons, which the cache refuses with a 400: its key parts allow letters, digits, dots, dashes and underscores.)

 Why does a settled payment write two ledger rows, one positive and one negative?

A single "amount paid" column can drift without anyone noticing. Balanced entries make errors visible: a sum that is not zero means a bug.

 The process crashes right after the settle transaction commits and before fee.paid is published. What is lost, and what fixes it?

The in-memory bus forgets everything on a crash. The outbox makes "the payment is recorded" and "the event will be published" one atomic fact.

 How does Greenfield allow instalments while Unity does not, without any if (school === …) in billing?

Per-customer behaviour belongs in configuration you can change and audit. The code only asks "is this allowed here?".

 What do 201, 202 and 402 mean for a payment response?

Distinct statuses let the client act without parsing text: show a receipt, show "we will confirm", or ask for another card.
