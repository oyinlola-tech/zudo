You tap "Transfer ₦5,000 to Tunde" in a banking app. Where does the backend fit in?

The phone only sends a request and shows the answer. The backend, on another computer, checks who you are and whether you have the money, updates both accounts, saves the change, and replies. The screen you tap is the front end.

 In a to-do app, which part is the front end?

The front end is the part you see and touch. The tasks themselves live on another computer, where the backend stores them and answers the app's requests.

 ZudoJS is a framework. What does that mean?

A framework gives you ready-made parts so you do not write everything from zero. ZudoJS is written in TypeScript and gives you parts for backends. The program that runs JavaScript on a server is Node.js.

 Why does the academy teach HTTP, databases, security and even your own mini framework before ZudoJS?

ZudoJS is the destination, not the starting point. After building the pieces by hand, the framework is no longer magic: you understand the problem each part solves.

 How is the academy organised, from biggest to smallest?

Six tracks group 19 levels. Each level is a course, a course is split into modules, and a module into lessons. Tiers are labels on courses and lessons, not a level of the structure.

 In which order do the six tracks come?

Each track builds on the ones before it: reasoning, then the language, then types, then backend engineering without a framework, then ZudoJS, then production.

 What is level 1 about?

Level 1 is the Thinking track. You learn to reason about a problem before you write serious code.

 There are 19 levels but 20 courses. Why?

Every level is a course, except level 4, which has two courses at the same level. So 19 levels hold 20 courses.

 Which levels form the ZudoJS track?

Levels 12 to 15 are ZudoJS: fundamentals, application development, advanced systems and architecture. Levels 7 to 11 are Backend engineering, and 16 to 19 are Production.

 What is special about the Backend engineering track (levels 7 to 11)?

The Backend engineering track works with no framework, so you see every problem a framework solves before you meet ZudoJS in level 12.

 How is the Task API built during the academy?

You write the Task API by hand with Node.js at level 4. From level 12 you rebuild it with ZudoJS, add a database, users, permissions, events, background jobs and tests, and later deploy and monitor it.

 The BookStore API is written in TypeScript with no framework. What is that for?

Building a real backend by hand, with PostgreSQL, authentication and tests, shows you the repeated work a framework removes. The capstones are ShopFlow and the SaaS platform, not the BookStore API.

 Why do you build your own mini framework at level 11?

Once you have written a small container, router, middleware and validation yourself, you can read any framework, ZudoJS included, and understand what it does.

 Which two capstones end the academy at level 19?

ShopFlow is a complete commerce backend. The SaaS platform is a production-grade system that many customer companies share safely. A capstone is a final project that uses everything you learned.

 Which of these does a course page NOT show?

A course page shows the level, track, tier, time, prerequisites, your progress, the outcomes, the project, the modules and the checkpoint. There are no accounts, so there are no other learners' scores to show.

 How does the course checkpoint work?

The checkpoint draws 20 questions from all the lesson tests of the course. Get 16 right to pass. Five questions with 4 to pass is the lesson test.

 You fail a lesson test with 3 of 5. What happens next?

The test picks five questions at random from a bank of 30 to 50. A retry gives you five different ones, so you cannot pass by remembering answers: you have to understand the lesson.

 A lesson header says LEVEL 5 · LESSON 3 OF 23. What does it tell you?

The header gives the level of the course the lesson belongs to, and where the lesson sits in that course. Level 5 is the TypeScript course.

 What is the By the end of this lesson you can box for?

The objectives say what you will be able to do after the lesson. Read them first, and at the end ask yourself honestly whether you can do each one.

 What is the best way to use a Reason it out box?

The value is in doing the thinking before you see the code. Comparing your own answer with the worked reasoning shows you what you missed.

 An example is marked Node.js only. Why can it not run in the browser?

Browsers may not read your files or start servers. Examples that do must run on your own computer with Node.js.

 A box shows $ node --version. What do you type in your terminal?

A line starting with $ is a command, but you do not type the $ itself. The lines under it are what the computer printed back.

 Where is your progress (finished lessons, test results, checkpoints) saved?

There is no account and nothing is sent anywhere. Your progress lives in the browser you use.

 You did ten lessons on your laptop. Tonight you open the academy on your phone. What do you see?

Progress is saved in one browser on one device. Another browser or device starts fresh. Clearing the browser's site data also removes it.

 How does the Continue where you left off button know where you stopped?

The progress bars and the Continue button read what this browser has saved: which lessons you finished and which one you opened last.

 A course is labelled Foundation. What does that tell you?

Foundation means it is needed before you continue, like Programming thinking, JavaScript fundamentals and TypeScript.

 ZudoJS advanced systems is an Advanced course. What does that mean for you?

Advanced means "learn it when your application needs it". It is not forbidden, just not required before you move on.

 Sam has never written code. Where should Sam start?

Complete beginners follow every level. Each new word is explained the first time it appears, so skipping means missing those explanations.

 Chidi writes Python at work and has never used JavaScript. Where should Chidi start?

Chidi already thinks like a programmer, so level 1 can be skipped. The language is what is new, so he starts at /learn/javascript.

 Kemi builds websites with JavaScript and has never used types. Where should Kemi start?

People who know JavaScript start at /learn/typescript. ZudoJS assumes types, HTTP, databases and security, which Kemi has not learned yet, so jumping to level 12 would leave big gaps.

 Lee builds TypeScript backends every day. Where should Lee start?

TypeScript backend developers go straight to /learn/zudo-fundamentals. When a lesson uses something they skipped, it links back to the lesson that teaches it.

 You think you already know the material of a course. What is a quick, honest way to check before you skip it?

The checkpoint draws questions from every lesson of the course, so it tests exactly what the course teaches. Missed questions point to the lessons you need.

 What does this print? console.log("Hello! I am learning backend engineering."); console.log("Tracks:", 6, "Levels:", 19);

Each console.log prints one line, without the quotes. Several values separated by commas are printed on that line with one space between them.

 What does this print? console.log("Thinking"); console.log("JavaScript"); console.log("TypeScript");

A program runs from top to bottom. Each console.log prints its own line, in the order the lines are written.

 What does this print? console.log("I want to build an API.");

The quotes only mark where the text starts and ends. console.log prints the text inside them.

 What does this print? console.log("ZudoJS: levels", 12, "to", 15);

The commas in the code separate four values. console.log prints them with one space between each, and the commas themselves are not printed.

 What does this print? console.log(19); console.log("19");

The first line prints the number 19, the second the text "19". On their own they look the same: console.log does not show a text's quotes.

 What does this print? console.log("Task", "API"); console.log("Task" + "API");

A comma gives console.log two values, printed with a space between them. + joins two texts into one, with no space added.

 Course pages live under /learn/. What does this print? console.log("/learn/" + "typescript");

+ between two texts glues them together exactly, with nothing added or removed.

 What does this print? console.log("Tracks after TypeScript:", 6 - 3);

Numbers without quotes are real numbers, so 6 - 3 is calculated first and gives 3. Only text inside quotes is printed as written.

 What does this print? console.log("ZudoJS"); console.log("zudojs");

Text is printed exactly as written, capital letters included. The computer does not "fix" your spelling.

 A checkpoint has 20 questions. What does this print? console.log("Pass mark:", 16, "of", 20); console.log("Allowed mistakes:", 20 - 16);

Two console.log calls give two lines. 20 - 16 has no quotes, so it is calculated: 4 wrong answers still pass.
