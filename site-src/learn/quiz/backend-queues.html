In a checkout, which step should stay in the request instead of moving to a background job?

The answer to the customer depends on the payment and the saved order. Everything that only needs to happen eventually (receipts, invoices, analytics) can be queued, so it is retried on failure instead of failing the checkout.

 What is the difference between a job and an event?

"Send the receipt for order 1001" is a job. "Order 1001 was paid" is an event: e-mail, warehouse and loyalty can each react, and a new feature can subscribe without changing the producer.

 Why does a worker claim a job (hide it for a while) instead of removing it from the queue?

Only an ack deletes a job. A lease (visibility timeout) makes a crashed worker's job reappear, which is what turns crashes into retries.

 What does at-least-once delivery promise?

Redelivery after a crash is how nothing gets lost, and it is also why duplicates happen. The consumer must be idempotent to turn that into an exactly-once effect.

 A vendor promises "exactly-once delivery". What should you still do?

Across a network, a consumer can always do the work and crash before its acknowledgement arrives. Exactly-once is achieved inside a system, or as exactly-once effect through idempotency, never as a promise you can skip.

 PDF jobs take about 40 seconds, and the visibility timeout is 30 seconds. What happens?

The queue cannot tell a slow worker from a dead one. The lease must be longer than a normal run, or long jobs must extend it with a heartbeat.

 Why do retries wait longer after each failure (exponential backoff)?

Short outages are handled by the first quick retries; longer ones by the later, spaced-out ones, without hammering the service the moment it comes back.

 1,000 receipt jobs failed at 12:00:00 because the mail provider was down. What does jitter add to their retries?

Without jitter, every job retries at 12:00:01, 12:00:03, and so on together: a new spike each time. Randomness turns the spikes into a steady stream.

 A receipt job has the address bola.example.com. What should happen?

No retry can fix bad data. Retrying wastes work and delays the problem; deleting hides it. The dead-letter queue keeps it where people will see and fix it.

 Which API answer should normally be treated as transient (worth retrying)?

5xx answers, timeouts and 429 (too many requests) usually pass. Most 4xx answers mean the request itself is wrong, so the same request will fail again.

 What should a team do with the dead-letter queue?

Dead letters are work that did not get done: receipts not sent, points not credited. They are an inbox for engineers, not a bin.

 What is a poison message?

It might be malformed data or a bug that one specific payload triggers. maxAttempts and the dead-letter queue stop it from looping.

 Which consumer is naturally idempotent?

Setting a value to the same thing twice leaves the same result. Adding, sending and transferring change something each time, so they need deduplication.

 Why must the "processed message" record be written in the same transaction as the loyalty-points update?

Both writes commit together or not at all. Any split between them opens a window where a crash causes a duplicate or a loss.

 In the lesson's consumer, what does INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING message_id tell the code?

The primary key lets exactly one insert succeed. The others hit the conflict, return no row, and the consumer skips the effect.

 The effect is an e-mail through an outside provider, so it cannot share your database transaction. What is the best available protection against duplicates?

With an idempotency key, the provider itself ignores a repeated request. Without one, a rare duplicate remains possible, which for e-mail is usually acceptable.

 The checkout saves the order and then adds a job to Redis. Redis is unreachable for that one call. What is the result?

Two systems cannot be updated in one transaction. This "dual write" problem is what the transactional outbox solves.

 How does the transactional outbox avoid losing messages?

The order and its message commit together in the one system that has transactions. Publishing becomes a separate, retryable step.

 What does FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED do in the relay's query?

Several relays (or workers) can then share one table safely. The same query is the heart of a PostgreSQL-backed job queue.

 The relay publishes three outbox rows and crashes before its transaction commits. What happens next?

The relay is at-least-once too. That is why it derives the job id from the outbox row id, and why consumers are idempotent regardless.

 Why does each subscriber of order.paid get its own queue?

Separate queues mean separate retries, backlogs and dead letters. E-mail keeps flowing while the warehouse catches up.

 With retries and several workers, can a consumer rely on jobs finishing in the order they were enqueued?

A job that is retried after 8 seconds finishes after jobs added later. Where order matters, carry a version and ignore older updates, or use a broker that orders per key.

 What should a "send receipt" message contain?

Small payloads avoid stale copies and keep sensitive data out of queues, which are logged and inspected. Validate the payload in the consumer: it is outside data.

 Which metric best shows customers are waiting too long for background work?

A queue can hold many jobs and still be fine if they are fresh. The oldest job's age is the delay a customer actually feels. Watch it together with the failure rate and the dead-letter count.
